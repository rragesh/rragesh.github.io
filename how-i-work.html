How I work.

High ownership, honest communication, and a high quality bar. I do my best work when the outcome is clear, the relationship is built on trust, and I have the autonomy to find the simplest reliable way forward.

For hiring teams and collaborators, these are my working commitments: how I make decisions, take responsibility, and contribute to a team.

Working principles Team fit

What you can expect from me.

People first. Process next. Tools last.

Building robots taught me to think in systems. Becoming a founder meant learning that the team is part of the system, too.

That’s why the people-first philosophy of DevOps resonates with me: understand the people, shape the process, then choose the tools. It helped me grow from solving engineering problems to building a company—and remains my starting point whenever I join a team.

Autonomy with a high quality bar.

Give me the outcome, context, and constraints, and I’ll take ownership of finding the path. I make progress and trade-offs visible. Quality means reliable behavior, appropriate verification, and work another engineer can understand and maintain.

Trust that grows through the work.

I value human relationships as one of the most powerful sources of long-term leverage. Trust is built by keeping commitments, sharing context, and being honest when something is difficult. I want a collaboration to become stronger and more effective over time.

Direct communication, with respect.

Living and working in Germany has made me direct about opinions and requests. I say what I think, explain why, and make the next step clear. Expect specific feedback and early conversations about risks. I welcome the same candor in return.

Accountability for the outcome.

Good or bad, I own my decisions and the work I commit to. When something misses the mark, I surface it, explain what happened, and focus on putting it right. Credit is shared; responsibility is explicit.

Fail fast. Learn while the cost is small.

I test uncertain assumptions early through small, reversible experiments. The goal is to find useful evidence quickly and change course when needed. Production changes still deserve verification, review, and a recovery path. Fast learning and dependable delivery belong together.

Keep it simple.

I prefer the smallest design that meets the real requirements. I add complexity when a constraint justifies it and make that trade-off clear. A system the team can explain, operate, and repair is a better foundation for growth than one that depends on a single person understanding it.

Reason from first principles.

I start with the outcome, physical constraints, user needs, and available evidence. I make assumptions explicit and work through what must be true before choosing an architecture or tool. That helps the team solve the actual problem instead of inheriting unnecessary complexity.

Be an optimistic contrarian.

I believe worthwhile problems can be solved, and I’m willing to question an accepted approach when the reasoning is weak. I bring an alternative to test, listen seriously to objections, and change my mind when the evidence changes. The point is a better result.

Build leverage that lasts.

I look for code, automation, reusable tooling, and documentation that keep creating value after the initial delivery. The aim is to leave the team more capable, with useful systems and knowledge it can build on. This is where individual engineering effort becomes a shared capability.

Are we a good fit?

Best-fit environment
Clear goals, high trust, room for independent judgment, and a team that welcomes candid feedback and evidence-led decisions.
What I bring
Ownership from understanding the problem through implementation and handover, with attention to technical quality and the people who will operate the result.
What we should agree early
What success means, which decisions I can own, where we need alignment, and how we will balance learning speed with delivery quality.

These are my stated working preferences and commitments. The experience and projects on Work provide the context for a conversation about fit.
