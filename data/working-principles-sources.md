# Working principles and current-work copy

## Owner-provided principles

The owner supplied these eight values: autonomy and high-quality results; trust and the importance of human relationships; direct opinions and requests shaped by life in Germany; accountability for good and bad outcomes; failing fast; simplicity; first-principles thinking; and optimistic contrarianism.

`how-i-work.html` translates these into self-described working commitments and observable collaboration expectations. It does not present them as independently verified traits, endorsements, psychometric results, ratings, or guarantees of future performance. The fit summary and practical examples are editorial expressions of the owner’s stated values.

## Ideas drawn from The Almanack of Naval Ravikant

At the owner’s request, the page also draws on the following material from Eric Jorgenson’s published collection. The copy is paraphrased and linked, not represented as a quotation or endorsement. The additional ninth principle, “Build leverage that lasts”, applies the book’s code/knowledge leverage idea to reusable engineering systems and documentation.

- [Play long-term games with long-term people](https://www.navalmanack.com/almanack-of-naval-ravikant/play-long-term-games-with-long-term-people): trust, reputation, and relationships compound through repeated collaboration.
- [Take on accountability](https://www.navalmanack.com/almanack-of-naval-ravikant/take-on-accountability): visible responsibility includes the consequences of failure as well as success.
- [Find a position of leverage](https://www.navalmanack.com/almanack-of-naval-ravikant/find-a-position-of-leverage): code and knowledge can multiply the value of work; independence is paired with responsibility for outputs.

The owner’s first-principles and optimistic-contrarian preferences are not falsely attributed to a specific passage in these sources.
