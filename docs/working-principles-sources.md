# Working principles and current-work copy

## Owner-provided principles

The owner supplied these eight values: autonomy and high-quality results; trust and the importance of human relationships; direct opinions and requests shaped by life in Germany; accountability for good and bad outcomes; failing fast; simplicity; first-principles thinking; and optimistic contrarianism.

The owner later added the DevOps philosophy of people over process and process over tooling, describing it as instrumental in becoming a founder, bootstrapping a startup, and contributing to teams of different sizes and types. He approved “People first. Process next. Tools last.” and its two-paragraph founder narrative as the opening working principle.

`how-i-work.html` translates these into self-described working commitments and observable collaboration expectations. It does not present them as independently verified traits, endorsements, psychometric results, ratings, or guarantees of future performance. The fit summary and practical examples are editorial expressions of the owner’s stated values.

## Personal-refinement direction

The owner asked for a site that reflects the person behind the work, primarily for like-minded peers, and identified the existing presentation as too impersonal and formal. The revised homepage introduction draws on the principles above: people-first engineering, simplicity, candor, and sharing knowledge. The conversational introductions and reading invitations paraphrase existing public material; they add no personal interests, employment details, achievements, regional client claims, or new engagement scope. The approved story and all ten substantive working principles remain intact.

## Ideas drawn from The Almanack of Naval Ravikant

At the owner’s request, the original drafting also drew on the following material from Eric Jorgenson’s published collection. The copy is paraphrased, not represented as a quotation or endorsement. The additional ninth principle, “Build leverage that lasts”, applies the book’s code/knowledge leverage idea to reusable engineering systems and documentation. The owner subsequently asked to remove the public influences block and its navigation link; these references are retained here as development provenance.

- [Play long-term games with long-term people](https://www.navalmanack.com/almanack-of-naval-ravikant/play-long-term-games-with-long-term-people): trust, reputation, and relationships compound through repeated collaboration.
- [Take on accountability](https://www.navalmanack.com/almanack-of-naval-ravikant/take-on-accountability): visible responsibility includes the consequences of failure as well as success.
- [Find a position of leverage](https://www.navalmanack.com/almanack-of-naval-ravikant/find-a-position-of-leverage): code and knowledge can multiply the value of work; independence is paired with responsibility for outputs.

The owner’s first-principles and optimistic-contrarian preferences are not falsely attributed to a specific passage in these sources.

## Current stealth-startup experience

The Work page’s current-experience block restates the owner-provided capabilities already published in the approved personal story: software-factory infrastructure, CI/CD and release automation, verification, an in-house OTA system and its CE conformity work, ISO 9001/CE process leadership, release notes, and engineering KPI reporting. Value is described through the capabilities delivered, without adding operational figures or invented before/after metrics.

The current employer, actual position, program names, exact employment dates, private contact details, and internal operating metrics remain excluded from content and structured data. The page makes no claim to a certification award, certifying body, or personal professional credential.
