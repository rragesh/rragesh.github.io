## Positioning

**Audience:** Robotics founders, CTOs, and engineering teams in the EU and US moving from pilots into production fleets.

**Customer problem:** Deployments depend on manual steps and individual engineers; it becomes difficult to know what is running, judge rollout health, and recover when an update fails.

**Value:** A repeatable path from software release to robot deployment, with visibility, recovery workflows, and ownership inside the customer's team.

**Differentiation:** Hands-on experience across robot systems, release infrastructure, fleet operations, and company-building. The offer connects software delivery with the realities of operating physical robots.

These are engagement goals and mechanisms, not quantified guarantees.

## Make each engagement's outcome explicit

### Deployment Readiness Assessment

**Customer outcome:** Know what to fix before the next rollout.

The customer receives a release-to-robot workflow map, prioritized operational gaps, a sequenced implementation roadmap, and a review with their engineers. Those deliverables make the assessment useful as a standalone engagement. Any implementation is separately scoped; an assessment is not a mandatory prerequisite when the required work is already clear.

### Production deployment engineering

**Customer outcome:** Put an agreed deployment system into operation.

Connect release automation, OTA updates, monitoring, and recovery to the customer's existing infrastructure. Agree the implementation scope and review criteria, then include documentation and engineering handover so the team can operate and maintain the result.

### Fractional Robotics DevOps leadership

**Customer outcome:** Give deployment priorities and decisions a clear owner.

Work with founders and CTOs on technical direction, infrastructure investment, release standards, customer commitments, and realistic delivery milestones. Define the mandate and decision-making responsibilities upfront.
