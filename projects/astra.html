ASTRA: giving robots a shared view

Amrita University · India

A robot on the ground and a robot in the air see different parts of the same place. ASTRA explored how those perspectives could be connected in a multi-robot system.

From individual machines to a connected team

ASTRA was a security-oriented research prototype combining ground and aerial robots for observation and information sharing. Its central engineering question was how several machines could contribute to a shared picture of their surroundings.

That required more than putting cameras on moving platforms. Sensor readings, robot movement, and communication had to fit together so information from one part of the system could be useful elsewhere.

Connecting sensing, movement, and communication

The ground platform brought together cameras, distance sensors, satellite positioning, and an inertial sensor that measures movement and orientation. Mapping software helped the robot represent its surroundings and estimate its position within them.

ROS—the Robot Operating System—connected the software components. Embedded controllers handled hardware inputs and outputs, while an onboard Raspberry Pi ran higher-level software. The prototype robots communicated over a local wireless network with a central computer.

What the prototype explored

The project combined remote observation, robot navigation, and the exchange of information between machines. Its tests used robots close together in a low-interference wireless environment.

The work is an example of multi-robot integration: giving separate machines a way to exchange useful information while bringing their sensing and control software into one system. The demonstration and original project document provide a closer look at that prototype.
