My story/Rover years

An eight-wheeled rover with a different way over obstacles

University Rover Challenge · Utah · 2015

A wheel can only climb so far before the vehicle’s shape gets in the way. This student rover explored what happens when some wheels can also be lifted on leg-like supports.

Keeping a rover moving on uneven ground

The rover was developed for the Mars Society’s University Rover Challenge in 2015, which brings student engineering into a Mars-like landscape. Its design addressed the connected problems of getting over obstacles, maintaining wheel contact, and keeping the body stable.

I worked on system integration as part of the rover team and co-authored the published research. The complete project brought the drive mechanism, a robotic arm, sensing, and control into one platform.

Wheels with an extra way to move

The design combined four rocker wheels with four wheels mounted on adjustable, spider-like supports. A rocker-bogie arrangement lets connected wheel supports pivot as the ground changes. Powered supports added a way to raise or lower selected wheels relative to the body.

Springs and dampers absorbed movement and helped maintain contact with the ground. The idea was to give the rover more freedom to position its wheels while managing the weight and stability of the rest of the machine.

From a design model to a physical prototype

The team developed computer-aided design models, analysed the mechanism’s motion and structural loads, and built a working prototype for experiments. The published paper records that the design passed the University Rover Challenge’s Critical Design Review.

The wider rover design also included a robotic arm and stereo vision, which uses two camera views to estimate depth. The papers and demonstrations below show how the mechanical design fitted into that larger robotics system.
