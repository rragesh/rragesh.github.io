Taking the rover from the drawing board to the ground

University Rover Challenge · Utah · 2015

A drawing can explain how a mechanism should move. A physical rover makes its weight, wheel contact, and coordination visible. This page collects field-trial material from our 2015 project.

Why testing the whole machine matters

The eight-wheeled rover combined adjustable wheel supports with suspension, sensing, control, and a robotic arm. Testing brought those parts together under the weight and movement of the physical machine.

For a reader new to robotics, the useful questions are straightforward: which wheels are touching the ground, how does the body move as the supports change position, and how do the separate motions work together? Those are the kinds of questions field testing helps an engineering team examine.

The design and the footage tell different parts of the story

The accompanying system paper describes the rover’s mechanical design, electronics, sensors, cameras, and control architecture. It also documents the construction and experimental testing of the prototype. I contributed to system integration and am a co-author of the paper.

This page complements the rover design overview with a recorded field-trial demonstration. Together, the two pages connect the idea on paper to the work of building and testing a real robot.
