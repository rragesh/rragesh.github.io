DHRUV: a closer look at hazardous spaces

Amrita University · India

Before entering a potentially dangerous area, people need information. DHRUV brought environmental sensing, cameras, and a robotic arm together on a remotely operated rover.

Send back information from a distance

An industrial incident can leave responders with basic questions: what is in the air, what can be seen, and what can be reached? DHRUV was designed as a rover prototype for gathering information and supporting remote inspection in that setting.

The rover combined gas sensors, thermal cameras, and a movable arm. Together, they offered ways to observe conditions and interact with objects while the operator controlled the machine from a distance.

Several kinds of sensing on one platform

The sensor set covered gases including carbon monoxide, methane, and ammonia. Readings were presented through a web-based interface, making the measurements available to the operator as the robot explored.

Thermal imaging added a view of heat patterns, while infrared camera feedback and ultrasonic distance sensors supported navigation. A five-degree-of-freedom arm—an arm with five independent movements—positioned a gripper for remote manipulation.

Making the information reach the operator

A Raspberry Pi computer and an ATmega2560 microcontroller, a small controller for electronic devices, handled onboard processing and hardware control. ROS, a framework for robot software, carried control commands and telemetry: the sensor readings and status information sent back from the rover.

The engineering task was to make mobility, sensing, manipulation, and the operator interface work together. DHRUV illustrates how a mobile robot can bring several kinds of information to a person who needs to understand a place before entering it.
