Questions about working together.

Clarity on fit, scope, and ownership before an engagement begins.

When is your involvement most valuable?

When deployment is becoming a constraint on the business: pilots are moving into customer environments, fleets are expanding, or release and recovery work depends on a few people. I focus on the infrastructure and engineering practices needed to make that next stage repeatable.

Which engagement should we start with?

Start with an assessment to clarify risks and priorities, production engineering to deliver an agreed system, or fractional leadership for ongoing technical direction. We choose the engagement around the outcome you need.

How will you work with our engineering team?

I work with your engineers on the agreed scope, using your robot platform, deployment environment, and existing workflows as the starting point. We define access, decision-making responsibilities, and review milestones upfront, so ownership is clear throughout the engagement.

How are timing and fees determined?

Each engagement is scoped individually. The work required, system complexity, access, and level of involvement shape the proposal. We agree deliverables, milestones, responsibilities, and fees before starting; implementation and ongoing leadership are scoped separately from an assessment.

What happens at handover?

Implementation includes documentation and an engineering handover, covering the key decisions and workflows your team needs to operate and maintain the system. We agree handover expectations and any ongoing support as part of the scope.

How does this relate to Robotair?

I founded Robotair. This page describes working directly with me on deployment engineering and technical leadership. For a Robotair-specific inquiry, mention the product in your message so we can clarify the scope.

Let’s talk about what you’re building.

Tell me about your robot platform, the deployment challenge you’re facing, and your next milestone. We can start by discussing fit and the right scope.
